# Evidence and confidence

Historical records can be incomplete, obsolete, or contradictory. Separate what the records say from your conclusions.

## Classify the evidence

- **Direct evidence** explicitly states a reason, requirement, incident, or decision. Quote or accurately paraphrase it with an adjacent citation. Check who made the statement, when they made it, and whether it concerns the code under investigation.
- **Indirect evidence** supports a conclusion without stating it. Name the records and explain how they fit together. Several records repeating one original statement do not provide independent corroboration.
- **Inference** is your interpretation. Show the supporting evidence and plausible alternatives. Use language that matches the strength of the evidence.
- **Unknown** means the available records do not answer the question. Name the material gap and a useful next source or person when one is identifiable.

A direct statement proves that someone made it, not that it was correct or remains valid. Do not turn a tentative account into a firm explanation because it is easier to read.

## Avoid misleading conclusions

- Code behavior, style, and test assertions do not establish an author's intent.
- A change made near an incident does not prove that the incident caused it.
- An error disappearing in a release does not identify which change in that release fixed it.
- Missing records do not establish that a concern was absent.
- A repeated pattern may result from copying rather than a deliberate convention.
- A user's suggested explanation is a hypothesis. Check the evidence against it.

Preserve contradictions with citations. Different records may describe different parts of the work, or one may be stale or incorrect.

## Check the answer

- Every claim about motivation has a citation or a clear inference label.
- The phrasing does not imply more certainty than the evidence supports.
- Contradictory accounts and material unknowns remain visible.
- Each empty search names its scope and query, so the reader can judge what the empty result means.
- Unavailable sources are distinguished from searched sources with no relevant findings.
- Unnecessary detail does not hide the answer to the original question.
